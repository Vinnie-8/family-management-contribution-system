## Conclusion

Talking to these four family members — a young professional who just wants paying his dues to be as easy as tapping his phone, an older Treasurer-adjacent relative tired of doing money math in his head, a Secretary who dreads the December scramble to compile a year's worth of records before the AGM, and a busy 30-something who's genuinely afraid of missing family events because work crowds everything else out — made one thing clear: this was never really a request for an "app." It was a request to be relieved of a mental load that's been quietly falling on whoever happens to be paying attention, or holding the role, at any given moment.

What stood out most was how consistent the underlying need was, even though the four people asking for it couldn't be more different in age, role, and comfort with technology. Nobody asked for something flashy.

!!! info inline end "Example"
    Every feature built from here on should be traceable
    back to a real thing a real family member said, 
    not just to what seemed technically interesting to build.

They asked for reminders that actually reach them, numbers they don't have to double-check by hand, reports that write themselves instead of being assembled under deadline pressure, and confirmation that a payment really went through. Small, specific, entirely reasonable asks — and together, they form a pretty complete blueprint for what "done well" looks like for this system.
